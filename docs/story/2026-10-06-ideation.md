# Story ideation — Oct 6, 2026
*Early thoughts from today's brainstorm. Not canon. Do not treat any of this as settled.*

**Thesis.** "The work is mysterious and important."

**The frame.** You're running a TV channel; later, the league. An ant farm you set up and hope works out. Ratings are the score.

**Phase 1: the channel (now).** Pick 3 drivers. They're your talent, your sponsored stable. Goal: the most exciting TV. Vectors like excitement and chaos are your programming strategy, and each one needs narrative identity, not just a number: excitement for the god of spectacle, chaos for the viewers who want crashes, whatever the sponsors want this week. Your audiences are spinning plates: gods, viewers, sponsors, each wanting a different show. Achievements and tags are content that entertains the masses.

**Phase 2: the commissioner (later).** League-wide metrics and goals. Spin up leagues for entertainment. Control fates. Powers expand: your three, your channel, the league. The fun is building the perfect little house of cards: the perfect league for these fans, the most competitive field, the most crashes, for whatever clientele.

**The paperclip arc.** The escalation IS the story. You start picking favorites and end up running the show, and at no point does the game ask you to stop. Management keeps moving the target: competitive balance this week, sponsor satisfaction the next, spectacle the next. The numbers go up, you feel good, and you have no idea what any of it means. (Ref: Universal Paperclips, 2017.)

**The moral texture (DCC).** The drivers think they're doing a sport. They're also little AI guys, maybe not sentient. Both things sit there, unsettled on purpose. Putting real-feeling people through this for entertainment, without questioning it, should make you feel a type of way about entertainment itself. The discomfort is the product.

**The voice.** Management. Cheerful corporate encouragement, "good job," subtly wrong. Never explains, only encourages and re-targets.

**The texture.** Severance eeriness: a weirdly ancient terminal, office-culture rituals, everyone acting like the racing obviously matters, nobody saying why. The F1 parody skin is the obfuscation layer over a mechanically solid racing sim.

**Bible rules.** Internal-only, never player-facing. Mysteries are load-bearing: what the work does, whether any of it is real, whether the drivers are sentient, what Management wants. Never answered, but the world may accumulate evidence (curated leaks). No gambling: fantasy is bragging rights, sponsorships are capitalism critique.

**Open questions.** Bits vs. real (lean: unanswerable). Does the commissioner know the drivers believe it's real? Narrator specifics beyond Management. Gods as audiences (story now, mechanics parked).
